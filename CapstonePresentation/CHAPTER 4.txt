Table 4.1
SUS Calculation of All Respondents for the Integrated Digital Platform for Donations, Event Reservation and Digital Bulletins for The Diocese of Iligan
The Iligan Diocese Integrated Digital Platform for Donations, Event Reservation, and Digital Bulletins had a usability score of 58.6 out of 100, which falls under the "Below Average" category. Although the system works, it doesn't work as well for all users who must work with it.
The platform has real strengths worth recognizing. Parishioners appreciate how the different features connect together, and many find the basic interface approachable. People genuinely want to use it on a regular basis specifically for reading bulletins and keeping abreast of parish activities. For these simpler jobs the system delivers on its promise. The issues are caused when customers are attempting to make a move of booking a service or donating. These are fundamental actions which are unnecessarily complex and earn lowest satisfaction scores. The PayPal payment process is a labyrinth for most, the booking form is overbearing with too many questions, and quite a few give up before achieving their objective. This is especially frustrating since most of the Filipinos who use digital payments are using GCash and PayMaya, neither of which the system supports in any way. Already, almost 60% of customers need assistance from younger relatives or tech-savvy acquaintances just to navigate the site. This reliance is exactly counter to the purpose of the digital accessibility bringing parish services more accessible to everyone. And a mere 41% are confident in using the system independently. There's also a learning curve right out front with most users stating they had to invest a good amount of time and effort before they became comfortable. 
The older parishioners especially have a difficult time comprehending the system and the system lacks the ease-of-use that many systems most people will be able to pick up quickly independently. Parish administrators and staff rated the system as 87.5 (Excellent) but they're working with it on a daily basis and are familiar with all the shortcuts and workarounds. In contrast, regular parishioners that make up 96% of users gave it a 57.4 (Below Average). That's a 30 point difference in experience. The system is wonderful for the two people operating it but is a bit of a chore to the 48 parishioners attempting to use it for the very first time or occasionally. At 58.6, is where the foundation lies people need this system to work. They just wish it to be where they are. This means that payment options that Filipinos literally use every day in the Philippines (GCash, PayMaya), are plugged into simple booking forms which just query what it needs to, and are made with bigger fonts and buttons (for older eyes), with improved, clearer steps, and making fewer steps that individuals have to go through to do regular uses on the platform. Narrow that 30-point gap between administrators and regular users and this site becomes what it can be, a bridge that introduces a community of the whole parish, not merely the tech-savvy minority. The ability is there it simply must be made more inviting to all who enter its digital doors.


Table 4.2
SUS Score Calculation for Citizens Respondents Integrated Digital Platform for Donations, Event Reservation and Digital Bulletins for The Diocese of Iligan
The Integrated Digital Platform for Donations, Event Reservation, and Digital Bulletins of the Diocese of Iligan also got a usability rating of 57.9 out of 100 from 48 parishioners, falling under the "Marginal" category. The system is functional, but it isn't functional for all those who must use it. That 10-point disparity short of the industry average of 68 speaks volumes: individuals want this platform to work, but it's making them do too much work.
The site does have genuine strengths to be admired. Parish members enjoy having all of it mesh together in one system where they can read bulletins, reserve services, donate, etc. There is a feeling of consistency to the interface, which is helpful when you're struggling to recall where things are. It is easy enough for many to do mundane things with and most of the time the individuals really do want to utilize it on a regular basis, specifically to keep up with the goings-on in the parish. For those more rudimentary transactions, the system delivers as advertised. The issues start when people begin to actually use the site for something book a mass intention, make a confession reservation, contribute a donation. These simple actions appear to be overly complicated, and the satisfaction ratings are at the lowest point at about 48-50 points. Almost half of the respondents indicated that the platform is too clunky to use like fighting against it, as opposed to working with it. The same percentage reported that they had to learn a great many things before they could even initiate. Only about half felt certain of using it on their own. Not only is this frustrating it's a hindrance that keeps individuals from being able to interact with their parish community in purposeful ways. 
What the data says is disturbing: Half of users feel that they'd need technical support simply to be able to use the system effectively. That translates to for every two churchgoers yearning for a way to book a service or donate, one is most likely to either be phoning a younger relative, begging a friend who is tech-friendly or simply giving up. Such reliance undermines the essence of online parish services to be more accessible to all and not only those who are tech-friendly. If your grandmother is not able to book a seat for Christmas mass by herself, the system is not doing what it was designed to do for the community. 
What is particularly concerning is the gap between the ability and usability. The platform is good on consistency (82.25) and integration (71.25) the technical base is sound. The issue is not what the system can do, the issue is how difficult it makes the people work to be able to do it. Users don't require additional features: they require more explicit routes to the features that already exist. They require guidance when they are lost, reassurance when they doubt and forgiveness when they go wrong. 
Currently, the platform is kind of assuming everyone knows what they're doing and all that, and that kind of assumption is causing half of the community to struggle. To stem that gap, the platform must go to the people. If users are first-time users, begin with a good welcome an onboarding sequence that takes users through the essentials without overwhelming them. Include small helpers throughout the path: tooltips for explaining unfamiliar words, "What's this?" buttons with context, soft prod that assist the users on their way to what they intend to do Streamline the routes to frequent tasks - fewer clicks, clear labels, reduced cognitive load Make video tutorials that show-not tell-reads them: how to donate or reserve a service, using as plain (as possible) language that anyone can follow. Above all else, try the system out on real parishioners (of varying ages and comfort levels) and observe where they trip up and hear their frustration. At 57.9, the foundation is there. People are willing to have this system function (this frequency of use (68.25) confirms it). They're not complaining that they're not receptive to digital solutions, they're wanting something that will not leave them feeling inadequate or confused. The 10-point shortfall beneath industry norms is actual people giving up halfway through, actual donations not submitted because the form was too difficult, actual relationships with parish life that never occurred because the technology intervened.
Fill that gap with targeted enhancements in direction, streamlining, and accessibility, and this platform becomes what it can be: a bridge that unites the whole parish community, not an obstacle that only the computer literate can navigate. The potential is there it just has to be more inclusive, more tolerant, and more human for all who come through its electronic doors.

This Gap Isn't Just a Number; It Is a Warning Sign A truly usable system should not require getting absorbed daily into something to feel it perfectly fine. The comfort of a staff member means that he/she has put in the required time to overcome the learning curve of the system but does not totally mean it is well designed. The excellent experience of the staff member proves that the platform works; it just needs to work for everyone and not just the one managing it.
